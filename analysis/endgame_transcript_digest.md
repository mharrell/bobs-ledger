# Endgame advice digest — mined from the transcript corpus (2026-09-20)

Source: ~32 endgame-dense transcripts read in full from `meta/transcripts/`
(136 files total), swept for top-4-conversion advice. This is the evidence
base for engine_coaching.md Plan 5 (top-4 conversion at 5k+). Video ids are
transcript filename stems. Corpus authors are mostly Shadybunny (self-ID'd
educator), Zorgo, Sliders, Beater Babbit, Matsuri, Jeef, Alutemu.

## 1. Positioning

- **Rock-paper-scissors vs the three endgame board types** (big stats /
  token / poison-scam): vs big-stat boards, huge minions go all the way
  BACK so they don't "face plant into the vamps"; exception — vs a known
  weak-only-taunt opponent, position 1 is safest, but never the same
  placement two fights running ("your opponent might adapt and taunt a
  venomous minion instead"). vs token comps, taunted reborn minion in
  position 1 "to get the reborn value by attacking first." (yhult8FLYM4)
- **Mech tech:** "consider putting your Deflect-o-Bot second. This way you
  can reset the shields right after the blaster's effect"; weaker mech
  first when the enemy Leroy is their only win condition. (yhult8FLYM4)
- **Positioning outranks stats in the last seconds:** "you could spend the
  rest of your turn here to fix positioning because any stats you're about
  to gain is pointless compared to making sure you don't mess that up."
  Final-fight start-of-combat chain order: biggest minion far left, doubler
  next, amplifier next. (DqPYeFZch0c)
- **Taunt count is a dial:** vs a cleave lead, sell down to exactly one
  taunt — "with only one taunt, he guarantees that he goes into the
  Leroy." Cleave is "vulnerable to getting Leroy." (a-jI8aSEdSs)
- **Position BECAUSE of the next opponent:** "heads-up positioning based
  on his next opponent... if he puts the cleave first, there's a reasonable
  chance he gets a cleave twice" — the scout-driven placement loop Plan 5
  wants to automate. (RxWAI0GLZsg)
- **Deflect-o-Bot discipline:** "If we're alive next fight, Deflecto goes
  up"; taunting Deflect-o-Bot early is a misplay ("kill all your
  automatons at the start of the fight"). (KjKTgeAppVI)
- **Anti-Sindorei (taunt+reborn dragon):** opponent "puts the Sindoreis
  further back, so very good adaptation — nullifies this taunt"; counters:
  taunted Leeroy, Kel'Thuzad blocks it. (itEcEPfqfbE, LuyDQHg9nmM)
- Shield-bumping placement ("put this first to bump shields"), reborn-value
  magnetize targets ("not on Sky Golem — then I won't get the reborn
  value"), and attack-order micro ("golden Bile attacking early is better
  than pulling it"). (hZPiwtZvK2E, KjKTgeAppVI, ag9BCU2zGRM)

## 2. Scaling races

- **THE rule — "there is a point at which it's too late to scale":** other
  lobbies have hit their scaling cards and are ahead in the stat race;
  past that point, play the next fight: "Buy the Leroy earlier. Get armor.
  A board of 100s is better than 50/50s, but they both get destroyed by a
  board of 300s. Leroy and Venoms give you a chance to win... I've had so
  many second places where my board was the fourth strongest at best,
  simply because I identified my position early and started conserving
  health." (yhult8FLYM4)
- **Or press the advantage:** "If you're on 31 health and your board is
  decent, do not stay an extra turn on tier 4" — snowball economy instead.
  (yhult8FLYM4)
- **Decision point ~turn 8-9 (first 10-gold turn):** decide push-for-first
  vs settle-for-second. "I pretty much never play for anything below
  second... preserve enough health to lose against the person that's going
  to win, and add extra cards like Leeroy or any poison insta-kill."
  B-tier comps are "stronger at that moment, lower ceiling." (GeuRrFYtsZo)
- **HP thresholds gate leveling:** don't level to 5 below ~15 HP; "turn
  seven needs 26+ health; turn eight and beyond, 31 is preferable — two
  turns to stabilize." "Leveling to tier 4 past turn 7 is under-leveling —
  not a spot to play for top four." (yhult8FLYM4, A0EqnsBkClo)
- **Level only with a target list:** "before you press the level button,
  mentally list units you'd be happy buying. Too short a list = expand it
  or don't level." "A turn-seven menagerie jug is a solid upgrade; a
  turn-12 one is a huge waste of two gold." (A0EqnsBkClo)
- **"Just barely over the bar" (the tempo-overshoot model):** "if you're
  already winning the fight, investing more into the board by not leveling
  is incorrect. You want to be just barely over the bar and put the rest
  into economy/levels." — the quantifiable core for lever 2 (growth-aware
  forecast). (GeuRrFYtsZo)
- Late game is execution, mid game is the test: "very often it's just
  executing the composition you're already in — buy a Leeroy and play it...
  these things are easier to replicate. Mid game is where you are really
  tested" (80% of the work). (GeuRrFYtsZo)
- No-money-no-scaling = scam: "money but no huge scaling — that's almost
  always scam as an end game." (0sRiOxp9wVE)

## 3. Divine shield / reborn / keyword weighting

- **Shields are matchup-relative:** "Mechs do well vs big-stat comps (high
  damage) and are resilient vs poison scam due to shields, but struggle vs
  token comps — shields get much less value there." (yhult8FLYM4)
- **Keywords follow the win condition, not habit:** "why would you shield
  your deathrattle thing? You want that to die. But this isn't a
  deathrattle comp anymore." Take the venom dark gift over value "if he
  goes against a really strong opponent." (FJyKGdqRj4Q)
- **Poison > venom when it survives:** "poison is way dirtier — it can hit
  something and survive and still have poison"; reborn+poison is the top
  endgame package. (itEcEPfqfbE)
- **CONCENTRATE stats, add scam — do not spread:** "Such a common
  misconception: 'spread my stats to play around Leroy.' Don't. Just play
  your own Leroy. Two really big guys plus Leroys and a spore... spreading
  out the stats is how you end up smaller than your opponent." (bvo8chc5SZ0,
  ag9BCU2zGRM, tZ5_JYBO_cI)
- **Don't trade established keywords for marginal shop value:** keeping
  Soul Rewinder on board "cuz the gain is not worth the effort — you're
  losing a lot of stats for a minor benefit." (hEmCWcqORCo)
- The shield/poison answer kit: Blaster + Selfless Hero + Titus packages
  "destroying enemy Leroys and shields while shielding your own board."
  (yhult8FLYM4, a-jI8aSEdSs)

## 4. Final-fight planning

- **Scout and classify:** "you must scout the lobby and know what your
  opponents are playing — three types of boards, rock-paper-scissors
  balance" (big stats / token / poison scam), each with a prepared
  counter-tech list. (yhult8FLYM4)
- **Scan fast, commit the turn to ONE unit:** "make sure he scans his
  opponent quickly, knows which unit he's looking for to end on, then
  thinks about that one unit while spamming cards." (RxWAI0GLZsg)
- **Top-4 stakes = damage cap framing:** "It's a top four, damage cap is
  off — we should be looking to end on a strong unit." "End on" becomes a
  first-class purchase decision. (a-jI8aSEdSs, hEmCWcqORCo)
- **Ghost fights are free rolls / transition windows:** "against the ghost
  we're going to switch because you have to be less afraid of scam."
  (weU-QaPXaA8, -KoaHL8l6hU)
- **Transition gates:** a late pivot is only correct "if you genuinely feel
  you will lose without the venom — you're introducing risk: play down a
  unit, wait longer"; and "you can prep for the transition and never get
  there — also annoying." (itEcEPfqfbE, weU-QaPXaA8)
- **Cut scaling only when scaling no longer matters:** "If you sell
  Balinda, you cut your scaling by a third... unless the guy is
  unoutscalable" — hedge with "bank a Leroy just in case the guy is an
  absolute monster." (LuyDQHg9nmM)
- **Pre-buy insurance before an expected loss:** "if you expect to lose and
  could survive another one with 10 armor, pre-buy the armor immediately."
  (LuyDQHg9nmM)
- **Survive the payoff turn, don't tilt:** "he's not going 'it's over, I'll
  just level to six'... he's looking at 'what do I need to do to not die
  here?'" (BPG_wv3q1E0)

## 5. High-roll vs preserve

- **The cardinal warning:** "Don't get consumed with high roll. A good
  position can get you killed — you feel invincible and start doing stupid
  things, where the sensible thing one or two turns would have been fine."
  (kRdHCWj8ooE)
- **Greed must have a payoff:** "you want your greed to actually have a
  payoff... if by buffing his board he wins last round, it means he can
  just go to six now. Everything's connected." (og2NYl_Ri7U)
- **Choose the greed moment deliberately:** "he chose his greed moment
  where he played down to just escapes and hooktusks." (DqPYeFZch0c)
- **Winning boards earn the rule-break:** "Everything you learn tells you
  not to roll there. But he has a coin in his hand... once you're good
  enough, you can break the rules a bit." (ag9BCU2zGRM, WWr05j-D5cU)

## Addendum — Jeef trio (user-found, 2026-09-20): 5qtIFm2-zK4, tjSsXWdgTvE, foZx60jbC74

Three Jeef videos (self-described rank-1 NA, first to 20K MMR) — the
highest-signal endgame sources in the corpus now.

### 5qtIFm2-zK4 — "10 tips to climb to 10K" (the post-5k playbook)

- **Pick a turn to stabilize** (tip 3): the 15-HP damage-cap breakpoint
  is "two lives" — stay above it at all costs; stabilize cards won't
  make the final board but are the difference between −15 and −0.
  At 16 HP unstabilized, take the fight-winner over the mega-scaling
  piece; at 16 HP stabilized, take the scaling.
- **Enablers ≠ commit points** (tip 4): only commit to a direction
  you've ENABLED (stabilization naturally enables one); the commit
  point is usually the giga scaling engine. One Turquoise Skitterer
  opens beasts; it does not commit them.
- **Large improvements only** (tip 5): useless→addon and addon→core are
  the meaningful upgrades; skip everything smaller (a Bonker next to a
  Titus setup is too small).
- **Concentrate stats** (tip 6), with two reasons: 300/300s value-trade
  multiple midsize units, and concentrated boards can SELL support to
  slot endgame units (spreading makes every unit unsellable).
- **Go all-in at the right times** (tip 8): sell Bran/Jakgar for
  Leeroy/Blaster when death is next ("those two spots net you 50 MMR");
  and the aggressive form — scout-said-the-rival-is-the-threat → "sell
  my scaling unit to eliminate them right now, guaranteeing first."
- **Prepare the final board EARLY** (tip 9): Bran/Jakgar are the worst
  ENDING units; anticipate the Leeroy and buy it in advance, matchup-
  gated (good vs stats, unnecessary vs beasts/undead).
- **Pivot rules, two conditions** (tip 10): (a) into BETTER scaling,
  (b) from a genuinely good spot (high HP + economy) — "if you can't
  guarantee you'll be stronger within two turns of pivoting, don't
  pivot." Explosive scalers (demons/murlocs) are pivot targets;
  end-of-turn-scaling comps are not.

### tjSsXWdgTvE — "Leveling guide, 2025" (the deepest leveling source)

- Level to 2 on turn two 80–90% of games; the exceptions are
  1-cost-hero-power curves (hero-specific, named per hero).
- To tavern 3: "always curve out with your shops" — 3-on-3 is fine
  ("you're also guaranteed to die staying down buying two one-drops");
  never past turn 5. Not-trash = strong board-impact pairs, synergistic
  combos, good plays with a non-token start.
- To tavern 4: 4-on-4 is an emergency (~5% of games, "you'll go eighth
  a good percentage of the time — it's just better than buying trash").
  Turn-5 with 7 gold: prefer LEVEL over rolling for two buyables
  ("rolling for two is not consistent"). Turn 6 vs 7: is the board
  FULL? are you STABLE? if neither → buy tavern-3 stabilizers instead.
- **To tavern 5/6: "not at all about the turn number"** (t7 earliest,
  t12 late). Two questions: (1) "What am I leveling FOR?" — must be a
  named target (a specific card, the level-to-6 plan, or triples);
  invalid: "because I feel like it" or +1 damage. (2) "Will I die if I
  level?" — pool-aware per the 15-HP cap. Plus the named-units-list
  test (too short a list → expand it or level) and "a turn-7 menagerie
  jug is solid, a turn-12 jug is a waste."
- Coach parity note: the named-list test ≈ our comp shopping list, and
  "will I die" ≈ the DYING/FRAGILE gates — external validation of the
  roll-vs-level walk's shape.

### foZx60jbC74 — "Direction: enabling vs committing" (the comp-tracker spec)

- The doctrine: **enabling ≠ committing ≠ forcing.** Tempo sticks
  (Hungry Snapjaw) are not directions; "leaning" via enablers then
  committing at the commit-point piece is the average-placement-max
  line; forcing is only consistent in anomaly-inflated metas.
- **The per-tribe enable/commit table** (machine-encodable for
  comp_target's next evolution): Mechs enabled by Scrap Scraper,
  committed by Beatboxer/Jakgar; Dragons enabled by Smuggler/Bran
  (never commit Kalecgos without); Pirates can't commit without Titus;
  Murlocs enabled by Bran + Primalfin; Elementals enabled by Genie/
  Wraith, committed at Rock Rock; Quilboar: Boogie/Spearhide enable →
  Piper commits (t5), Piper enables → 6-drops commit (t6); Slamma
  beasts enabled by Sly Raptor → committed by Slamma; self-damage
  demons enabled by Wrath Weaver + Soul Rewinder; Nagas enabled by
  Orgozoa, committed by Lord of Gains/A.F. Kay; Undead enabled by Sore
  Loser, committed at Champion of the Primus/Sister Death Speaker.
- **Pivot pieces cost resources** — hold them only when far enough
  ahead ("you may just be griefing yourself").
